########## HUNK 4 : HEAD ##########
  /* ── Chapter bands ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   * The two — and only two — background tones a long page alternates between
   * so consecutive sections read as separate chapters. Used by .ae-band in
   * global.css; the History page is what composes them.
   *
   * These are deliberately the SAME values as --bg-primary / --bg-surface
   * above, not new colours: dark mode already had a legible alternation and
   * this refactor must not move a single pixel of it. They exist as their own
   * names because LIGHT mode needs a different step size (see the light block)
   * and because a band is a page-composition decision, not a surface level —
   * a section on --band-lift still puts --card and --bg-elevated on top of it.
   *
   * The step between them is ~1.07:1 in luminance, or ΔL* ≈ 3.5. That is the
   * number the light pair is tuned against, so the two themes alternate with
   * the same perceived strength rather than one shouting and one whispering. */
  --band-deep: #07091c;
  --band-lift: #0d1035;

########## HUNK 5 : HEAD ##########
  /* ── Chapter bands (LIGHT) ────────────────────────────────────────────────
   * The light theme was already alternating in the right DIRECTION — the
   * deeper tone first, the lifted one second, exactly as dark does — but at
   * --bg-primary #f7f8ff against --bg-surface #ffffff the step was ΔL* ≈ 2.4
   * with essentially no chroma difference. On a screen that is not a rhythm,
   * it is one flat white sheet, which is why the page read as unbanded in
   * light mode while the identical structure read clearly in dark.
   *
   * #f0f2fb restores the step: ΔL* ≈ 4.4 against white, so it lands just past
   * the dark theme's own 3.5 — near-white bands need slightly more separation
   * than near-black ones because there is no glow or halo helping the eye — and
   * it carries a real indigo tint, which is the second cue dark mode gets for
   * free from --bg-surface being the more saturated of its pair.
   *
   * ⚠ This is the DARKEST surface the light theme puts body copy on, so it is
   * the binding constraint for every muted text token. Verified at this value:
   *   --text-muted     #6366a8 → 4.70:1  (AA, with margin)
   *   --text-secondary #3b3f7a → 9.6:1
   *   --sky            #1d4ed8 → 6.0:1
   *   --gold           #b45309 → 5.2:1
   * Push --band-deep any darker and --text-muted is the first thing to fail —
   * it is already the tightest of the four. Re-measure before you do.
   *
   * NB: PALETTE_LIGHT_OVERRIDES.background in sceneConfig.ts mirrors this
   * value, so the 3D canvas and the band it sits in have no visible seam.
   * Change one, change the other.
   *
   * ⚠ #eceef9 IS THE FLOOR — this cannot go darker without breaking text.
   * --text-muted (#6366a8) is the binding constraint: it lands at 4.55:1 here,
   * and the next step down the ramp (#e8ebf7) puts it at 4.42:1, under AA, on
   * 11px labels. Deepening the band any further means darkening --text-muted
   * first, and that compresses the light theme's text ramp the same way the
   * dark one is already compressed. Measure before you touch it. */
  --band-deep: #eceef9;
  --band-lift: #ffffff;
  /* A real hairline, because two pale neutrals butting together need one.
   * Same value as --border-solid, at ~2:1 against the tone on either side —
   * a definite rule rather than a hint. A first pass at #c9cde8 measured
   * 1.36:1 and read as a smudge, which is the failure mode this whole change
   * exists to fix. */
  --band-edge: #b0b3e0;
